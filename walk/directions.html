Indoor in MapLibre: directions

A working list, grown from what a first-person walk through a real station actually hits. Last change 31 August 2026. It is deliberately incomplete: one line is added when a measurement supports it.

The demo it comes from: walking inside Gare de Lyon, built on unedited OpenStreetMap data (216 rooms, 383 walls and columns, 173 stairs, escalators and lifts, 128 doors, levels +2 to −6).

Method

Build the thing, walk through it, and write down only what the engine refuses to do. Each direction below holds items in one of three states: already native, correctly outside the engine, or missing, with a measurement. A missing item earns its own upstream issue with the numbers attached; this page never replaces that conversation, it only keeps the shape of the whole visible in one place.

The headline so far, and it is not what a proposal would assume: the style specification is close to sufficient for indoor. What is short is renderer and camera correctness, one blend primitive, and a written convention.

Direction A — the camera has to work at human height

Everything indoor happens with the eye 1.7 m above a floor, often below the ground, often looking up. That is the least exercised part of the camera.

Direction D — an immersive view needs labels that respect distance

Symbols are sized in screen pixels, which is right for a map seen from above and wrong from inside a building: a shop 60 m down the concourse is drawn as large as the door in front of you, and the view fills with names.

itemstateevidence
no camera-distance input for text-sizemissing text-size accepts zoom and feature inputs only, and the spec has no camera-distance expression. Worked around here by measuring the distance in the page and writing it onto every feature, then re-uploading the source as the camera moves.
knowing which point on the floor a label belongs topage drawn here as a dot and a stem; nothing in the engine ties an elevated symbol to its anchor visually.

What is not MapLibre's job

Named so the list above stays credible. Searching gl-js and the style spec finds no issue and no PR for any of these, and that is the right outcome. Upstream, "collision" means label collision, a different subject.

  • walls and columns stopping a body, floors you cannot walk off
  • routing between two points and across floors through stairs and lifts
  • turning OSM level=-4;-3;-2 into numeric bounds, which is the conclusion of style-spec#1843

How this list grows

Walk somewhere new in the demo, hit something the engine will not do, measure it, add one row, open one issue. No umbrella proposal: the shape lives here, the argument lives in each issue.
